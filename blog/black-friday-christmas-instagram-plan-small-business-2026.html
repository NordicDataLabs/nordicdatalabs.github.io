Black Friday to Christmas: An Instagram Plan for Small Businesses (2026)

8 October 2026

Holiday Instagram is easy to waste when every post is invented on the day. In 2026 the dates are fixed: Black Friday is Friday 27 November, Small Business Saturday is Saturday 28 November, Cyber Monday is Monday 30 November, and Christmas Day is Friday 25 December. This plan runs week by week from 12 October to the end of December, for freelancers, service businesses, and small product sellers.

Write the offer before you design a post

Write one sentence and repeat it until 30 November: the offer, who it is for, the start, and the end. Use your real price. A service might open January bookings at a November rate across those four days. A product seller might run a winter bundle only while set-aside stock lasts. A mixed shop might reduce gift vouchers now and ship later orders in January. If the price or the contents are still open, do not tease it. Point every November post at one checkout link, booking page, or DM.

The countdown, week by week

Two or three feed posts a week is enough until mid-December. If that is too many, six posts cover the season: the tease, the reveal, 27 November, 30 November, your cutoff, and the thank-you.

12–18 October — Tease the season, not the price

Say that something is coming, and leave the price off. Post a carousel and a 15-second Reel: the three November dates, what each day is for you, and “details in November.” Use your own voice, or audio cleared for a business account. In stories, ask whether people want gifts, bookings, or a restock.

19–25 October — Open an early-access list

Promise the first look on Monday 16 November, and hold the public post until the next day. Ask people to reply LIST, or use a pinned comment or a form you already have. One feed post is enough. Write down the exact promise next to the send date.

26 October – 1 November — Post something that stands alone

Publish one useful post with no offer on it: a checklist, a how-to-choose, or the problem your November offer solves. Design the rest of November this week and keep the files in a folder.

2–8 November — Put the three dates on one graphic

The three days do different jobs. Show that before you ask for a sale.

Date What to post
Friday 27 November, Black Friday The main offer and one link.
Saturday 28 November, Small Business Saturday Who you are. Not a second discount.
Monday 30 November, Cyber Monday Last day of the same offer, or a digital extra.

Write the real closing time, such as 18:00 on Monday 30 November, and keep it.

9–15 November — Reveal the offer in one sentence

Message the list on Monday 9 or Tuesday 10 November. A day later, post four facts: what it is, who it is for, a start on Friday 27 November, and an end on Monday 30 November. Stories that day cover price, what is included, and how to buy or book.

16–22 November — Gift guides and familiar questions

For products, post five slides: one person, one product, and only what you can fulfil. If you are stocking the peak, track units on hand against units already promised. The Bolde Logistics Inventory Tracker is a €19 Excel workbook for that, with no macros. For services, offer a voucher, a booked session, or a starter package, and note which need no shipping. Answer timing, contents, and uncertainty in stories.

23–29 November — Black Friday and Small Business Saturday

Monday 23 to Wednesday 25: repeat the sentence, with no new bonus. Thursday 26: a tomorrow story, and a cutoff only if the carrier confirmed it. Friday 27 November: the offer, the link, and what the buyer receives — not a made-up review. Saturday 28 November: where you work, who replies, the same link, no second code. Sunday 29: a story recap.

30 November – 6 December — Cyber Monday, then a new subject

On Monday 30 November, put the closing time in the first line, then take the offer down. Extending it in a late story teaches people to wait. From Tuesday 1 December, post three things you can still deliver without a last-minute courier: a gift, a file, or a booking.

7–13 December — A last shipping date you have confirmed

Publish a Christmas cutoff only after the carrier, or the method you really use, confirms it. Put that date in the first line and on the graphic, once this week and again the day before. If you do not ship, say so, and give a January booking date or same-day digital delivery instead.

14–20 December — What can still be a gift

One post lists vouchers, digital files, local pickup, and January bookings. A second lists your hours from Monday 21 December through that weekend. Do not add a new offer this week.

21–27 December — A quiet Christmas week

Monday 21 to Wednesday 23: hours, pickup, and a thank-you. Thursday 24: open or closed. Friday 25 December: a thank-you only if you post, with no link and no offer. Leave Saturday 26 and Sunday 27 alone.

28–31 December — Thank you, and a short year in review

One carousel: something you delivered, something you fixed, and something you learned. Thank people who bought, booked, or sent a friend, and skip any numbers you would rather not share. Do not launch a new offer on 31 December.

Batch it in Canva so the month does not run you

Two sittings beat a new design every morning. In Canva Free, make one 1080×1080 frame with your colours, type, logo, and a corner for the date. Duplicate it through Cyber Monday and change only the headline, the date, and one line. For stories, duplicate the frame at 1080×1920 and move the text above the reply bar.

Batch in the week of 26 October, then make the gift guide and cutoff graphic in the week of 7 December. Leave Christmas week empty. If a frame takes longer than the caption, stop.

Ten captions to rewrite in your voice

Replace the brackets. Keep a date only when it is true for your plan.

  1. Teaser. Black Friday is 27 November, Small Business Saturday is 28 November, Cyber Monday is 30 November. No price today. Reply LIST for the first look.
  2. Early access. First look on Monday 16 November, before the public post. One sentence: what it is, who it is for, when it ends. Reply LIST.
  3. The three dates. Friday 27 November: the offer. Saturday 28 November: the business, not a second discount. Monday 30 November: last day, closing at [time]. Save this.
  4. Offer reveal. [One sentence.] Starts Friday 27 November. Closes at [time] on Monday 30 November. Link in the caption. Price and what is included are in today’s stories.
  5. Gift guide. One person per slide. I only listed what we can fulfil. If a slide sells out, it comes off the same day.
  6. Black Friday. Friday 27 November. [One sentence.] You get [three items or steps]. Link below. The same offer runs through Monday.
  7. Small Business Saturday. Saturday 28 November. [Where you work.] [Who replies.] Same link as yesterday. No second code.
  8. Cyber Monday. Last day. Monday 30 November, we close at [time and timezone]. After that, [offer name] comes off the page.
  9. Last order date. Last [method] order for Christmas arrival: [date you confirmed]. After that, pickup, a file, or a voucher.
  10. Year in review. This year we [specific work], fixed [specific snag], and learned [specific lesson]. Thank you for buying, booking, or referring. January is for [one plan].

Questions that come up every year

Do I need a different discount for each of the three days?

No. One offer across Friday 27 November, Saturday 28 November, and Monday 30 November is easier to explain and easier to fulfil. Use Saturday to talk about the business. Use Monday to state the closing time.

What if I do not ship physical products?

Skip the last-shipping posts. Use a booking cutoff, a gift voucher, or same-day digital delivery instead. Say which one you offer, and the date it stays available.

When should I announce a last order date for Christmas?

When the carrier, or the method you actually use, has confirmed it. Post that date in the week of 7 December, and again the day before the cutoff. A guessed date creates refunds and apologies.

How often should I post in December?

Two or three feed posts a week is enough through 13 December. In Christmas week, stick to practical notes, and skip anything promotional on 25 December. The week of 28 December needs one thank-you.
