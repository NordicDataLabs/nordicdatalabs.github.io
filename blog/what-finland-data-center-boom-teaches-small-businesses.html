What Finland’s Data Center Boom Teaches Small Businesses About Systems That Just Run

Last updated: 8 October 2026

By Bolde team

Digital tools & templates

Within one month, Finland got two of the largest technology investments in its history. On 9 September 2026, Google said it would invest at least €13 billion in Finnish data centers in 2027 and 2028. On 8 October, the French AI infrastructure company Sesterce announced a campus costing more than €10 billion in Jämsä, on the site of a former paper mill. Cold weather and clean power explain part of it. The rest comes down to something Finns call sujuvuus: things simply run. Here is what a freelancer or a small shop can take from that.

What was actually announced

Google: at least €13 billion, four locations

Google calls it its largest single investment in Europe. The plan covers new data center sites in Muhos, Kajaani and Vaala in northern Finland, and growth in Hamina, where Google turned an old paper mill into a data center after buying it in 2009. Hamina is cooled with seawater, and its waste heat is designed to cover about 80% of the yearly heat demand of the town’s district heating network. Google says it chose the northern sites with the grid operator Fingrid and Business Finland, because the region already has spare grid capacity close to carbon-free power.

Sesterce: more than €10 billion in Jämsä

Sesterce plans to build on the old UPM Kaipola paper mill site. Construction of the first phase, a 200 MW liquid-cooled AI data center, is due to start in 2026, and a second phase would take the site to 600 MW. In the longer run, the company wants more than 1 GW of AI capacity in Finland, built on existing industrial sites. Sesterce says it already has an anchor customer but has not named it. According to the company, the reason for Kaipola is that the power connection, water systems, buildings and transport links are already in place.

And one honest footnote

Smooth does not mean anything goes. On 6 October, Finland’s Licensing and Supervisory Authority (LVV) told Google’s Finnish subsidiary to stop major site work at Muhos and Kajaani by 23 October at the latest, until the environmental impact assessments are finished. Planning and surveys can continue. Google said it had “fallen short of our own high standards in this instance” and would follow the agency’s guidance. That is part of the story too: the Finnish system is predictable because it enforces its own steps.

What sujuvuus actually means

Sujuvuus translates loosely as smoothness or flow. Nothing about it is flashy. In practice it means that the forms are online, the rules are written down, and a decision made today still holds next year. In the European Commission’s 2025 Digital Decade report, Finland scored 98.75 out of 100 for digital public services for businesses, against an EU average of 88.59. Starting a business scored the full 100.

Look at the two announcements above and the same pattern shows up three times:

  • Reuse what already exists. Both companies chose old paper mill sites with grid connections, water and roads.
  • Go where the capacity is. Google is building in the north, where there is more electricity than people use.
  • Grow in phases. Sesterce starts with 200 MW, plans 600 MW next, and only after that talks about a gigawatt.

None of that needs a €10 billion budget. It works the same way for one person with a laptop.

Five lessons for a small business

1. Build on what you already have

Data centers move into old paper mills because the hard parts are already built. Your business has an old mill too: posts that did well last year, a price list, the questions customers keep asking, a spreadsheet you half finished. Before you make anything new, list what you can reuse. A good post from last spring with a new hook is often your best post for next week.

2. Do the work where your capacity is

Google is building where the grid has spare power. Do the same with your week. If your best focus comes on Sunday evening, batch a week of content then instead of fighting for ten minutes every morning. Write captions in one sitting and make visuals in another. Planning beats daily inspiration because it puts the hard thinking in the hours when you actually have energy for it.

3. Grow in phases you can finish

200 MW, then 600 MW, then more. A small business can copy that order. Plan one week of posts, run it, and only then plan a month. Track your 20 best-selling items before you try to track all 400. The Free 7-Day Instagram Starter is built as that first phase: a one-week calendar, seven caption starters, and three visual references you recreate in Canva Free.

4. Make the routine boring on purpose

A data center is designed so that nobody has to be a hero at 3 a.m. Your systems can work the same way. Post on the same days each week, keep one sheet as the single source of truth for stock, and use one link for orders. When the routine runs on its own, your creative energy goes to the work that needs it.

5. Check the rules before you start digging

The halt in Muhos and Kajaani is a reminder that skipping a step costs more later. For a small business the steps are smaller, but they are just as real. Confirm a shipping date before you announce it, check that your Reel audio is cleared for business use, and count the stock before you promise it. Questions

What does sujuvuus mean?

It is a Finnish word for smoothness or flow. In a business sense, it means simple, predictable systems that work without daily firefighting.

Why are data center companies investing in Finland?

The companies point to available grid capacity, carbon-free electricity, a cool climate, and existing industrial sites such as former paper mills. Google picked its northern sites together with Fingrid and Business Finland, and Sesterce says it chose Kaipola for its existing infrastructure.

Is Google’s Finnish project still going ahead?

As of 8 October 2026, the regulator has ordered Google to stop major site work at Muhos and Kajaani until the environmental impact assessments are finished. Planning and surveys can continue, and Google has said it will follow the agency’s guidance. The Hamina and Vaala parts were not covered by the order.
