Q4 Checklist for Freelancers and Small Businesses: 12 Weeks to a Calm Year-End (October 2026)

Last updated: 10 October 2026

By Bolde team

Digital tools & templates · Based in Finland

December crowds work that belongs earlier: open invoices, a rate you meant to change, and posts written the night before. This Q4 checklist for freelancers and small businesses spreads that over twelve weeks, from Monday 12 October 2026 to Sunday 3 January 2027, with one main job a week.

The dates in the table are real. Leena, the illustrator in the examples, is fictional, and every euro figure is made up. Use your own numbers. This is not tax, legal or financial advice.

The guide is published on Saturday 10 October, so week one still starts on Monday.

Weeks run Monday to Sunday.

Weeks 1–2: Chase unpaid invoices before December

Start with money you have already earned. A reminder in October lands in a normal working week. The same note on 27 December often meets an out-of-office reply. In week one, list every unpaid invoice with its number, client, amount, due date, and whether you have already asked. Oldest due date first. In week two, send whatever is still missing, and pause new work for anyone seriously late.

In week two she sends a second note if they are still open. She waits to start the next illustration for the client who owes €1,200 until that invoice is paid, or a date is agreed in writing.

Keep a copy of what you sent, and the date. The guide on how to track invoices as a freelancer covers a simple sheet, an overdue flag, and reminder wording you can reuse.

Weeks 3–4: Review the budget against what happened

A budget is a guess from January. By late October you can check it with two columns: what you planned, and what happened. Count money that has arrived, and costs you have paid. Leave hoped-for payments off the sheet. Use January through September, or through October if those weeks are already entered.

Weeks 5–6: Set 2027 rates, and raise them in January

Decide next year’s price in November, and start charging it in January. A change left until the December rush is easy to drop, and the old rate then stays for another year.

Build the number from your costs and the hours you can really bill. The guide on how to calculate your freelance hourly rate uses take-home pay, a tax estimate, business costs and billable hours. Run it again if you last did it in the spring.

Leena’s fictional inputs are a take-home goal of €36,000, a made-up estimate that 30% of profit goes to tax and contributions, costs of €400 a month (€4,800 a year), and 880 billable hours. That 30% is only for the example. It is not a tax rate for any country. The steps:

  1. Profit needed before tax: €36,000 ÷ 0.7 = €51,429
  2. Add business costs: €51,429 + €4,800 = €56,229
  3. Divide by billable hours: €56,229 ÷ 880 = €63.90

She treats €64 as her floor, the rate at which the planned hours only just cover the year, and chooses €68 an hour for work booked from 1 January 2027. The €68 is her example, and a higher rate can also mean fewer yeses.

In the week of 16 November she tells current clients the new rate, the start date, and that anything already agreed for 2026 stays at the old price. She sends that as its own note, so January plans can use a real answer.

If you are registered for VAT, say whether the new price includes it. In Finland the general VAT rate is 25.5% (vero.fi). Check your own country’s rate with your tax office or accountant before it goes on a proposal.

Weeks 7–8: Plan Black Friday and December content now

Write the offer before the week it runs. In 2026, Black Friday is Friday 27 November (timeanddate.com), which is week seven on this list. If the price, the dates and the sentence are still blank that morning, the week will feel rushed.

You can run a discount, open January bookings, sell a voucher, or skip the sale and publish one useful post. A product business also needs a real stock check and a last day. If there is an offer, write one sentence: what it is, who it is for, when it starts, and when it ends.

Leena’s fictional studio skips the discount. On Friday 27 November she posts ten January illustration slots at the €58 she already charges in 2026, closing on Monday 30 November. She writes that post in October. December is two posts a week: one finished project, and one answer to a question clients already ask. The Black Friday to Christmas Instagram plan maps the season post by post. Give each topic an afternoon in the diary.

Weeks 9–10: Clean the client list and the follow-ups

Sort the list into three groups before the holidays: an active project, a January follow-up, or quiet. Quiet means no project and no real conversation for months. Keep the history if you want it, and take quiet names off the view you open every morning.

Leena’s fictional sheet has 22 names: 7 active, 6 for January, and 9 quiet. For each of the six she writes one date in the week starting 11 January 2027 and one sentence about why she is writing. She saves the sending for January, when people are back at their desks.

A simple CRM spreadsheet for freelancers holds a pipeline stage, a follow-up date and a short onboarding list, which is enough for these two weeks.

Weeks 11–12: A 2027 content calendar and the quiet admin

Week eleven is 21–27 December, and Christmas Day sits inside it. The one job is a rough 2027 content calendar: four lines, one per quarter. Captions can wait until the month they belong to.

  • January to March. Who you help, and how a project starts.
  • April to June. How the work is done, and finished examples.
  • July to September. Questions you would like to answer once, properly.
  • October to December. Next year’s offer, drafted in September while the calendar is still open.

Leena blocks the first Monday of each month in 2027 for one planning hour. The calendar holds topics and dates. Captions get written in that hour.

Week twelve, Monday 28 December to Sunday 3 January, is one sitting of admin. Then stop.

  • Download the year’s bank and payment statements.
  • Match them to the invoice list from weeks one and two.
  • Put receipts in one folder for each month.
  • Export your spreadsheets and save a second copy somewhere else.
  • Write down what you are still owed, and what you still owe.
  • Add any tax date your own tax office has already given you.

If you are self-employed in Finland, you file a business tax return after the tax year, including a quiet year. Confirm the due date in MyTax, as the Tax Administration’s instructions describe (vero.fi). This page is a planning aid. Follow the date on your own return, and finish the list above before the holidays.

Questions

What should a freelancer do first on a year-end checklist?

List every unpaid invoice, with the amount and the due date, and send the reminders in October. Money you ask for in October is easier to plan around than money you chase between Christmas and New Year.

When should a small business raise its prices?

Decide the new rate in November, tell current clients before the holidays, and use the new price on work that starts in January. Leave any price you have already agreed for this year as it is.

Does a small business need a Black Friday offer?

You need a decision, and a discount is only one of the options. Either write one offer with a start and an end, or decide you are not running one. In 2026, Black Friday is Friday 27 November, so the useful time to decide is October.
